## Problem-honing objective

The goal is to identify whether the Hyperkit problem is frequent enough, painful enough, and expensive enough to support adoption.

This note is meant to narrow, not expand, the problem. If the evidence points toward a narrower workflow than the current wedge, the wedge should narrow further rather than widen.

## Priority problem statement

The most promising first problem is:

Developers repeatedly experience workflow fragmentation when moving from idea to secure smart contract deployment, especially when generation, audit, simulation, and deployment are spread across different tools.

## Validation grid

### High frequency and high intensity

Build here if interviews show:

1. the problem appears in most serious shipping cycles
2. the delay is measured in hours, money, or missed release windows
3. teams already built workarounds or bought services to reduce the pain

### High intensity but low frequency

Monitor, but do not anchor the company here if:

1. the pain is severe only for rare protocol types
2. the issue appears only in edge-case deployments

### High frequency but low intensity

Do not build the wedge here if:

1. the issue is frequent but users do not pay to fix it
2. the issue looks like convenience rather than pain

### Low frequency and low intensity

Deprioritize completely.

## Strong proof signals

1. Repeated emotional language around slow, messy, or risky workflows.
2. Existing workarounds such as scripts, checklists, spreadsheets, or manual release gates.
3. Real spend on audits, simulation tools, deployment services, or contractor help.
4. Clear statement that one workflow stage is painful enough to remove first.

These are stronger than compliments, concept validation, or general enthusiasm because they point to real cost and repeated effort.

## Weak proof signals

1. Compliments without behavior change.
2. Generic excitement about AI.
3. Broad statements that smart contracts are hard.

## Reflection

1. Is the chosen problem frequent, intense, and costly.
2. Does the problem show up across more than one buyer group.
3. Would teams already spend time or money to reduce this pain without Hyperkit.
