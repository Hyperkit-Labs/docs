## Core anti-patterns

This file records the recurring mistakes the whitepaper and product narrative must avoid. Each anti-pattern is phrased as a decision trap rather than as a stylistic preference, because each one weakens the business case directly.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Hyperkit anti-patterns and corrections}
\begin{tabular}{|p{3.4cm}|p{4.4cm}|p{4.3cm}|}
\hline
Anti-pattern & Why it is dangerous & Correction \\
\hline
Over-engineering trap & Builds broad platform scope before proving one painful workflow is worth paying for & Keep the wedge narrow: contract generation plus integrated audit \\
\hline
Vanity metric trap & Replaces qualified usage with attention metrics such as waitlists, likes, or impressions & Track repeat workflow completions, qualified teams, and paid behavior \\
\hline
Single-user bias & Lets one loud user define the roadmap & Confirm repeated pain across multiple high-fit teams before expanding \\
\hline
Execution-proof confusion & Treats competition wins as demand proof & Keep milestone proof separate from user and revenue proof \\
\hline
Market-size inflation & Treats TVL or broad ecosystem value as software spend & Use developer counts for context and workflow-spend assumptions for market sizing \\
\hline
Pricing inflation & Prices above current developer-tool norms too early in the product life cycle & Anchor pricing to current developer-tool budgets and validate value capture through interviews \\
\hline
Support mismatch & Support and governance expectations grow faster than the support model & Scale async support, retention, auditability, and approval controls by tier \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

## What worked in prior feedback

The feedback identified the real danger correctly. Hyperkit was at risk of sounding technically ambitious before proving one painful workflow and one reachable buyer wedge.

## Areas for development

1. Keep every market claim tied to a buyer or workflow.
2. Keep every traction claim tied to evidence.
3. Keep the roadmap tied to measurable qualification rules.
4. Keep roadmap stages tied to risk retirement, not only to feature accumulation.

## Actionable correction

When revising future materials, ask these questions first.

1. Does this claim describe a proven fact, an assumption, or a target.
2. Does this metric prove demand, or only activity.
3. Does this feature help the narrow wedge, or distract from it.

## Reflection

1. Which current claims still sound larger than the evidence.
2. Which roadmap items would disappear if the narrow wedge failed.
3. Which metrics are still vanity metrics in disguise.
